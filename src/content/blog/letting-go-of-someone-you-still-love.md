Some goodbyes aren't loud. They don't come with slammed doors or final ultimatums.

They come quietly.

They come in the moments when you realize that staying costs more than leaving. That your peace is the price of their presence. That you've been shrinking yourself to fit into a love that was never meant to hold all of you.

## The Quiet Goodbye

I didn't block them. I didn't write a long message explaining why. I didn't make a scene.

I just... understood.

Understood that some people are meant to be loved from a distance. Understood that my healing required their absence. Understood that choosing myself wasn't an act of cruelty — it was an act of survival.

> Letting go isn't always about not loving someone anymore. Sometimes it's about finally loving yourself more.

## What Grief Taught Me

Grief is strange. You can grieve someone who is still alive. You can mourn a future you imagined together. You can feel loss for something that was never fully yours.

And that's okay.

The version of me that loved them — she was real. Her feelings were valid. But she also needed protection. She needed someone to finally say: *this isn't good for you.*

So I became that someone.

---

*We are always becoming. And sometimes becoming means unbecoming the person who tolerated less than she deserved.*
